## “Kahvi”

The nature of my name is that no one will pronounce it correctly on the first try — unless they’ve practiced beforehand.

Someone pronouncing my name confidently and correctly when they haven’t said it in a while makes me feel good. The act of caring to *remember* the pronunciation is very meaningful to me.

I appreciate when people ask me about the pronunciation too. I think it’s a decent conversation starter! And it gives me a chance to try one of my many phonetic devices:

\- [kahvi](https://kahvipatel.com/assets/pronounce.mp3) as in ***covenant***

\- [kahvi](https://kahvipatel.com/assets/pronounce.mp3) as in a ***covey*** of quails

\- [kahvi](https://kahvipatel.com/assets/pronounce.mp3) as in ***kahvi-lovey-dovey***

I’ll usually follow up with:

> Emphasis on the *uh* sound instead of *ah*. *Kuhv* as in *dove*.

As I’ve gotten older, mispronunciations bother me less. I’ve mellowed out about it.

In a short, fleeting interaction, I’ll use my easy name — **Roger**. This usually applies at Starbucks. I don’t feel at all like a Roger, but no one has mispronounced it so far.

I think everyone deals with this to a certain degree. There’s always going to be a gap between *how you’d like to be addressed* and *how you are addressed*. Nickname, middle name, emphasis here, ‘I go by Dave not David’, etc.

I feel grateful for my name because it sets me apart.
